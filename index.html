What a $149 Furnace Tune-Up Actually Covers

Your furnace has a job that starts on the first cold night of the year and doesn't let up until spring. Most of the time, it does that job quietly. When it doesn't, it tends to pick the worst possible night to quit.

We're Larkspur Home Services, a family-owned heating and cooling company in Denver. This winter, we're offering a furnace tune-up for $149. Normally it's $189. Here's what you get, what we look for, and how to decide if it's worth booking.

Why a tune-up, and why now

Ray Castillo started Larkspur in 2009 with one van and a phone that rang in his kitchen. After that many winters, he'll tell you this: "A lot of the no-heat calls we get in January are stuff a tune-up would've caught."

We don't have a percentage for that, and we're not going to make one up. It's just what our technicians see, year after year. A dirty flame sensor. A worn igniter. A clogged filter. Small things that are easy to fix on a calm Tuesday afternoon, and a lot less fun at 11 p.m. in January.

There's a scheduling reason too. Once the first real cold snap hits, our phones fill up with no-heat calls and the calendar gets tight. Book your tune-up before that and you pick the time that works for you. Wait, and the furnace gets a vote.

Put simply, we'd rather see you on a quiet afternoon in November than at midnight in January. You probably would too.

What we check

A tune-up isn't a quick look and a sticker on the side of the unit. Here's what one of our technicians does on every visit:

  • Inspect and clean the burners. Dirty burners don't heat evenly, and they make your furnace work harder than it should.
  • Check the heat exchanger for cracks. This part keeps combustion gases separate from the air you breathe, so we look at it closely.
  • Test carbon monoxide levels. We measure, so you know where things stand. No guessing.
  • Check the igniter and the flame sensor. These two are the usual suspects when a furnace won't start.
  • Test your thermostat. We make sure it's reading correctly and talking to your furnace the way it should.
  • Replace your filter. A standard 1-inch filter is included.
  • Give you a written report. You'll get a plain-English summary of what we found, so you're not left trying to remember what the technician said.

The whole visit takes about an hour. Older units can take a bit longer.

What to expect on the day

We give you a two-hour arrival window, and your technician will call when they're on the way. That's how we do every job, not just this one.

Our technicians wear shoe covers and clean up after themselves. You shouldn't have to vacuum after a furnace check.

Every one of our 12 technicians is a Larkspur employee. We don't use subcontractors, so the person in your basement works for us and answers to us. We're licensed and insured. [NEED: number of NATE-certified technicians, if we want to mention it]

What happens if we find a problem

We tell you. In plain English, with the price, before we touch anything.

If a part needs replacing, you'll know what it is, why it matters and what it costs. Then you decide. Nothing gets done until you say yes. If it can wait until spring, we'll say so. If a repair makes more sense than a replacement, we'll say that too.

No pressure, either way. That's how we work.

What a tune-up won't do

We'd rather be straight with you about this part too.

A tune-up won't make an old furnace new. If your system is getting on in years, a tune-up can tell you where it stands, but it can't turn back the clock. What it can do is give you an honest picture, so you can plan ahead instead of finding out on a cold night.

A tune-up also doesn't include repairs or parts beyond the standard 1-inch filter. If your furnace takes a different filter, or needs a part, we'll show you the price first. You can say yes, say no, or say "let me think about it." All three are fine.

And it's not for heat pumps. Those need a different kind of visit, and we're happy to set one up.

Who this offer is for

The $149 tune-up is for gas furnaces in homes across Denver, Lakewood, Englewood and Aurora. If you have a heat pump, that's a different visit. Give us a call and we'll walk you through it.

The tune-up is a good fit if:

  • You can't remember the last time your furnace was serviced.
  • Your furnace is making a new noise, or turning on and off more than it used to.
  • You've just moved in and don't know the system's history.
  • You'd simply like to go into winter knowing it's been checked.

A few things you can do between visits

You don't need to be an HVAC expert to look after your furnace. A few small habits help:

  • Check your filter. If it looks gray and dusty, it's time for a new one.
  • Keep vents and returns clear. Furniture and rugs over vents make your system work harder.
  • Give the furnace some room. Don't store boxes or paint cans right up against it.
  • Pay attention to new sounds or smells. If something changes, give us a call rather than waiting it out.

None of this replaces a tune-up. It just helps your furnace get through the winter between visits.

A few straight answers

How often should I get a tune-up?
Once a year, before it gets cold. That's what we recommend, and that's it.

How long does it take?
About an hour. A bit longer on older units.

Are you going to try to sell me a new furnace?
No. If your furnace has life left in it, we'll tell you. If a repair is the better call, we'll tell you that too. You'll always see the price first.

Is the carbon monoxide test the same as having a CO detector?
No. We test levels at the furnace during the visit. A CO detector keeps watch in your home all day, every day. It makes sense to have both.

What's included in the $149?
Everything in the list above, including a standard 1-inch filter and a written report. If we find something that needs fixing, we'll give you a separate price before any work starts.

FAQ

Questions agencies ask

Is client data safe?

The system runs inside your agency's own Claude account, not on a separate tool of mine. Briefs and drafts stay in your account, under your plan's data settings. On Claude Team and Enterprise plans, Anthropic doesn't train models on your content by default. For the free sample, send a brief you're comfortable sharing, or remove anything sensitive first.

Does a human review everything?

Yes. The system only writes drafts. It never publishes or schedules anything. Every run starts with a review checklist, and any fact it couldn't confirm is marked [NEED] and listed in the review notes.

What tools do I need?

A Claude account for your team. A paid plan (Pro or Team) works best for regular use. There's nothing else to install.

How long does setup take?

It depends on how many clients you start with. Setup covers building the system in your Claude, a voice profile for each starting client, and a test run your team reviews. We'll agree on a timeline on the first call.

What if the voice is off?

Tell me what's off. I update that client's voice profile and rerun the drafts. Monthly tuning is part of the $300/month.
